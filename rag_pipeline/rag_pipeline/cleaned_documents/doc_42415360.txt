Long Distance Non-Contact Dermoscopy: Technological Foundations, Clinical Applications, and Future Directions.

Recent advances in dermoscopic imaging have expanded diagnostic capabilities beyond conventional contact devices. Non-contact modalities, including short- and long-distance dermoscopy, preserve vascular morphology, avoid compression artifacts, and enable high-fidelity evaluation of pigmentary and structural patterns across diverse anatomical sites. Improvements in optical engineering, extended depth-of-field acquisition, and artificial intelligence (AI)-enhanced reconstruction now allow non-contact systems to approach the image quality and dermoscopic feature visualization of standard dermoscopy. When integrated with high-resolution total body photography, these modalities support automated lesion mapping, longitudinal monitoring, and early detection of melanoma and other skin cancers. This review synthesizes current evidence on the technological foundations and clinical implications of non-contact dermoscopy and proposes a conceptual framework distinguishing short-distance and long-distance dermoscopic imaging modalities within digital dermatology workflows.
